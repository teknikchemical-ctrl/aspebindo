# R2 — Energy & Mill-Economics Evidence for a BOO Thermophilic AnMBR POME Biogas Set

**Prepared:** 2026-10-16 · **Reference mill:** 45 t FFB/h, 20 h/d, 300 d/yr = **270,000 t FFB/yr**; POME 0.65 m³/t FFB = **585 m³/d ≈ 175,500 m³/yr**; plant ~1.09 MWe gross / ~0.96 MW net.
**FX used:** **IDR 17,948/USD** (BI JISDOR, 15 Oct 2026).

**Method note:** All evidence was gathered with WebSearch (standard + extended). WebFetch could not resolve any host from this sandbox (DNS failure), so I could not open primary PDFs such as the Perpres 112/2022 annex. Every number below comes from search-engine excerpts of the cited page. Confidence reflects that:
- **High:** several independent sources agree, or an official page is quoted.
- **Med:** a single credible source, or a secondary source quoting an official one.
- **Low:** a vendor or blog source, an OCR-garbled source, a stale source, or an analyst inference.

Values marked *[derived]* are my own arithmetic from the cited inputs.

## 1. Status-quo POME pond system + land application (LA)

### Design / land
- **Open ponding (or ponding + land application) is used by ~85% of mills because of low capex/opex and simplicity**: MDPI *Processes* 9(5):739 review "Palm Oil Mill Effluent Treatment Processes—A Review" (2021), https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/9/5/739 [Med]
- **Total pond-train HRT 100–120 d (acidification → anaerobic → facultative → aerobic/polishing); other reviews give 100–160 d**: ResearchGate copy of the same review (2021), https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352120649 [Med]
- **Anaerobic ponds 40–60 d HRT at about 5 m depth. A Kulai (Malaysia) mill design used 45 d anaerobic, 20 d facultative and 7 d + 7 d aerobic**: MPOB POEB 136 (Faizah et al.), https://palmoilis.mpob.gov.my/publications/POEB/poeb136-faizah.pdf; ResearchGate figure "pathway of POME treatment by the anaerobic pond system" [Med]
- **Land for ponds ≈ 5 ha for a 30 t FFB/h mill**: IOP Conf. Ser. Earth Environ. Sci. 365 012009 (2019), https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1755-1315/365/1/012009/pdf [Med]. A second Indonesian excerpt says **~7 ha for a 30 t/h mill** (Unsri/UMP conference papers, undated) [Low]. **A large mill may devote 12–18 ha** to effluent ponds (review excerpt) [Low].
- **Anaerobic pond volumes at four real mills: 17,231 / 51,824 / 56,000 / 81,720 m³**: ScienceDirect, "Effects of operational processes and equipment in palm oil mills on characteristics of raw POME: four mills" (2023), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772912523000271 [Med]
- *[derived]* **For 585 m³/d, a 100–120 d total HRT means about 58,500–70,200 m³ of pond volume. Scaling the 5–7 ha per 30 t/h figures gives ≈ 7.5–10.5 ha of pond land at 45 t/h, including embankments.** [Med]
- **Land application needs ~253 ha of plantation for a 60 t/h mill**: haisawit.co.id "Hitung cermat luas lahan LA dan JJK sawit untuk PKS kapasitas 60 ton per jam" (undated, ~2024), https://haisawit.co.id/news/detail/hitung-cermat-luas-lahan-la-dan-jjk-sawit-untuk-pks-kapasitas-60-ton-per-jam [Low]. *[derived]* That is ≈ 190 ha at 45 t/h.
- **Flatbed density 140–160 flatbeds/ha (3.2 × 1.4 × 0.4 m each). One-rotation application labour cost Rp 243,000/ha**: IPB *Bul. Agrohorti* 5(3):373, https://journal.ipb.ac.id/index.php/bulagron/article/download/16483/12095/ (c. 2017) [Med; stale]
- **Field LA dose 218.77 m³/ha/yr from the aerobic pond (case study)**: Indonesian university paper excerpt (jom.unri / UMP Klorofil) [Low]

### Capex of ponds
- **Pond system for a 60 t FFB/h mill: Rp 800 million – 1 billion capex and Rp 20–22 million/yr opex. LA capex is about equal to ponds, but LA opex is higher**: blog "Pemanfaatan Limbah Cair PKS untuk Land Application" (Oct 2014), https://zuelyzul.blogspot.com/2014/10/pemanfaatan-limbah-cair-pks-untuk-land.html [Low; stale]. *[derived]* At ~4%/yr construction inflation to 2026 (≈1.6×), that is Rp 1.3–1.6 bn ≈ USD 72–90k.
- **Vendor benchmark for a covered anaerobic lagoon (biological train only): USD 400–700 per m³/d of POME capacity, excluding land**: HydropureWater "Palm oil mill wastewater treatment system 2026 engineering guide", https://hydropurewater.com/blog/6651-palm-oil-mill-wastewater-treatment-system-2026-engineering-guide.html [Low]. *[derived]* × 585 m³/d = USD 234–410k.

### O&M of ponds and LA
- **Desludging a POME pond costs "about RM 100,000" per pond (text says "per pounds", probably a typo), excluding maintenance and utilities**: Jagaba et al., *Ain Shams Eng. J.* (2020), https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/75100/1/Jagaba_etal_ASEJ_2020_Sustainable_use_of_natural_and_chemical_coagulants_for_contaminants_removal_from_palm_oil_mill_effluent.pdf [Low–Med]. *[derived]* ≈ USD 22k ≈ Rp 0.4 bn per event.
- **Sludge accumulates 1,500–6,000 m³ per mill per year. Desludging is triggered when active anaerobic volume drops below the 45-day design HRT**: Infraconcrete geotube vendor page (Malaysia, undated), https://infraconcrete.co/geotube-dewatering-pome-palm-oil/ [Low]
- **Poor practice "without desludging at least monthly" is a common anaerobic-pond problem** (source gives no cost): Nugro Rahardjo, Neliti PDF, https://media.neliti.com/media/publications/244566-none-1a63f26e.pdf [Low]
- **Flatbed LA unit cost Rp 7/L (= Rp 7,000/m³) versus tanker-tractor LA Rp 19/L**: IPB repository, "Analisa Teknis dan Biaya Aplikasi Sistem Kanal Flatbed… PKS Condong, Garut", https://repository.ipb.ac.id/handle/123456789/60711 (c. 2012) [Med; stale]. *[derived]* 175,500 m³ × Rp 7,000 = **Rp 1.23 bn/yr** at old prices, or ~Rp 1.8–2.0 bn/yr after inflation.
- **LA can cut effluent treatment cost by 50–60%** versus full pond-to-river treatment: Nugro Rahardjo (Neliti) [Low]
- **LA fertiliser benefit: Rp 60–105 M/yr of avoided inorganic fertiliser and Rp 125–310 M/yr from higher FFB yield**: alimudinharahap.wordpress.com (2014) [Low; stale]. **The mill would lose this credit if LA stops, unless the AnMBR permeate is still land-applied.**
- **A 2021 life-cycle cost study notes "limited information… on capital and operational costs for POME treatment technologies"**: *Renew. Sustain. Energy Rev.* (2021), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364032121012533 [High as a statement of the data gap]

### Regulation, monitoring and compliance risk
- **Kepmen LH 28/2003 and 29/2003 (land-application technical guidelines and permits) were revoked by Permen LHK 5/2021 Art. 54. This left no specific LA quality standard.**: GAPKI (1 Jun 2024), https://gapki.id/news/2024/06/01/klhk-larang-limbah-cair-digunakan-sebagai-pupuk-di-kebun-sawit/ [High]
- **KLHK announced a plan (2024) to ban POME use as fertiliser on plantations. In Dec 2024 KLH said it would tighten LA quality standards; GAPKI proposed BOD < 5,000 mg/L (min 2,000 mg/L).**: sawitindonesia.com; langit7.id (Dec 2024) [Med]. **Final 2025–26 status not confirmed.** [Low]
- **Enforcement example: Inhu (Riau) environment office ordered a mill to dismantle LA pipes into community land because the LA had no permit**: elaeis.co, https://www.elaeis.co/berita/baca/pks-ini-diduga-buang-limbah-dengan-modus-land-application [Med]
- **The old 28/2003 framework required monitoring wells where groundwater is < 2 m deep, plus a 10–20% trial area**: toolsfortransformation.net copy of Kepmen LH 28/2003 [Med; regulation now revoked]
- **RKL-RPL monitoring/lab cost per year:** **no public figure found.** *Analyst assumption: Rp 150–400 M/yr for monthly accredited-lab effluent tests plus semester RKL-RPL reports. Confirm with a mill.* [Low]

## 3. Mill energy

- **Mill electricity use 17–19 kWh/t FFB in Indonesia; larger, more automated mills use more**: Indonesian source in a search excerpt; Thai mill 17 kWh/t (ResearchGate "Assessment and Improvement of Energy Utilization in CPO Mill") [Med]
- **Daily start-up electricity averages 18.7 ± 5.4 kWh/t FFB (Thai field survey)**: ResearchGate excerpt [Med]
- *[derived]* **Annual electricity use 270,000 t × 17–20 kWh/t = 4.6–5.4 GWh/yr, ~0.8–0.9 MW average while processing. Most of it comes from the fibre/shell-fired steam turbine at near-zero marginal cost.** [Med]
- **Whole-mill diesel use 0.53 L/t FFB (Jamaludin et al. 2018, cited in a simulation study)**: ScienceDirect, "Simulation and techno-economic evaluation of integrated palm oil mill processes" (2025), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666789425000698 [Med]. *[derived]* ≈ **143,000 L/yr** at 45 t/h.
- **Diesel genset specific consumption ≈ 0.42 L/kWh (0.304 kg/kWh). Investigate if a set uses > 0.5 L/kWh**: MPOB POEB 115 "A Diesel Power Plant", https://palmoilis.mpob.gov.my/publications/POEB/poeb115-ravi.pdf [Med; old sets]
- **Real case, PalmCo (PTPN IV) Terantam + Tandun covered-lagoon PLTBg: >2.6 M L diesel avoided in 2023–2025 and Rp 39.5 bn saved over three years. 293,000 m³ POME treated last year.**: InfoSAWIT (8 May 2026), https://www.infosawit.com/2026/05/08/palmco-optimalkan-limbah-sawit-jadi-energi-tekan-konsumsi-solar-hingga-jutaan-liter/; Jambi Ekspres (Apr 2026) [High]. *[derived]* ≈ 0.87 M L/yr and Rp 13 bn/yr across about 1.9 MW of PLTBg; implied Rp ~15,200/L.

- **Dexlite retail price Rp 23,700/L from 1 Sep 2026 (up from Rp 19,700); Pertamina Dex Rp 25,200/L. Unchanged for Q4 2026 (also Q2/Q3 2026 and Q4 2025).**: Kompas (13 Oct 2026), https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/10/13/083616126/tarif-listrik-per-kwh-13-18-oktober-2026-untuk-semua-golongan-pelanggan-pln; Metro TV; Bisnis (24 Sep 2025) [High]. *Note: these are base (LWBP) rates. ## 4. PLN purchase of biogas power (Perpres 112/2022) and grid access

- **PLTBg ≤ 1 MW ceiling (HPT): 10.18 USc/kWh × F for years 1–10, then 6.11 USc/kWh for years 11–30**: CNBC Indonesia (14 Sep 2022), https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20220914215339-4-372174/yang-dinanti-sudah-terbit-begini-aturan-tarif-listrik-ebt; Katadata (Sep 2022) [Med–High]. *A Slideshare copy of the annex instead shows 11.55 × F → 9.24 for 1 MW, but Katadata lists those numbers under PLTBm (biomass). Treat 10.18/6.11 as PLTBg.*
- **PLTBg > 1–3 MW: ≈ 9.29 × F (yr 1–10), from an OCR-garbled ESDM socialisation slide. The year 11+ value was not confirmed.**: Scribd "Materi Sosialisasi Perpres 112-2022", https://id.scribd.com/document/609079033 [Low]
- **Location factor F applies to stage-1 price only. Sumatra 1.10 and Kalimantan 1.10 (small islands 1.15; Riau Islands/Mentawai 1.20); Jamali 1.00; Papua 1.50.**: ESDM socialisation slides via Scribd; KPK review, https://cms.kpk.go.id/storage/4105/Kerentanan_Korupsi_Perpres_No_112_Tahun_2022.pdf [Low–Med; OCR]. **LPEM (May 2023) claims biomass and biogas do not use F; this conflicts with the slides.** [Low]. **Check Lampiran II.**
- *[derived]* **≤ 1 MW in Sumatra: 10.18 × 1.10 = 11.20 USc/kWh ≈ Rp 2,010/kWh (yr 1–10), then 6.11 USc ≈ Rp 1,097/kWh.** This is a ceiling; the PPA price is negotiated at or below it. [Med]
- **Excess power (kelebihan tenaga listrik) from PLTBg held under an IUPTLS (captive licence): HPT 7.44 USc/kWh, no escalation, negotiated**: Perpres 112/2022 annex excerpt (Kemenkeu JDIH PDF); CASE for SEA ESDM slides (2024) [Med]
- **Permen ESDM 5/2025 (in force 4 Mar 2025, revoking Permen 10/2017 jo. 10/2018): standard PPA terms do not apply to excess-power deals with IUPTLS holders, which are "by agreement of the parties". RE prices refer to the HPT (Art. 53(3)a).**: JDIH ESDM, https://jdih.esdm.go.id/dokumen/download?id=2025abspmesdm5.pdf; pasal.id [Med–High]
- **Historical PPA: PLTBg Pasir Mandoge (PTPN IV) 2×1 MW sold to PLN at Rp 1,018/kWh; capex Rp 42 bn; ~15 GWh/yr**: IDX Channel (c. 2017–18), https://www.idxchannel.com/economics/manfaatkan-limbah-dari-kelapa-sawit-ptpn-iv-pln-bikin-pltbg-2x1-mw [Med]
- **PPA and interconnection: most mills are remote with weak or no grid. "Negotiations with PLN on interconnection and tariffs are often long and bureaucratic", which creates uncertainty and deters investment.**: WUR policy brief "Pengembangan Biogas dari Limbah Cair Pabrik…" (c. 2025), https://edepot.wur.nl/704234 [Med]
- **Interconnection capex (MV line, 20 kV switchgear, metering): no Indonesian figure found.** *Analyst assumption: USD 50–150k per site plus USD ~20–40k per km of 20 kV line, paid by the developer. Confirm with PLN UIW.* [Low]
- **Power wheeling: still not law. RUU EBET has not been enacted. ESDM dropped power wheeling (Mar–May 2025) and proposed "PBJT"/network leasing instead; DPR says it aims to pass RUU EBET in 2026 (Aug 2026).**: Kompas Lestari (1 Mar 2025); Bloomberg Technoz; DPR e-media (14 Aug 2026), https://emedia.dpr.go.id/news/2026/08/14/ruu-ebet-terus-diprioritaskan-beri-kepastian-hukum-transisi-energi [High]
- **Regulatory flag (analyst, from UU 30/2009 structure):** if a third-party BOO company sells electricity directly to the mill, that is power supply to the public and normally needs an IUPTLU with a wilayah usaha. Captive use falls under the mill's IUPTLS. **The BOO structure therefore needs care.** Options: (a) the mill holds the IUPTLS and the developer is paid a treatment/O&M fee, with power "free" to the mill; (b) equipment lease; (c) the developer sells biogas, not power, to the mill's own gensets. [Low–Med; get legal advice]

## Implications for proving the mill saves money

**Bottom line:** The pond system alone costs the mill little cash. A BOO offer that charges a positive treatment fee and sells it as "cheaper than ponds" will not hold up against a mill's own accounting. The saving can be shown when it comes from three things together: **(1) diesel displacement at 2026 fuel prices, (2) avoided LA/desludging/compliance cost and regulatory risk, and (3) PLN export revenue that lets the developer charge a very low or zero fee.**

### 1. Where the case is strong
- **Diesel is very expensive in 2026.** Dexlite costs Rp 23,700/L and industrial HSD Rp ~19–25k/L. At ~0.3 L/kWh, diesel power costs **~Rp 6,600/kWh (≈ USD 0.37)**, about 6× the PLN I-3 tariff.
  - A mill burning 0.53 L/t FFB spends ~143,000 L/yr ≈ **Rp 3.0 bn/yr (≈ USD 170k)**.
  - Real Indonesian evidence supports this: PalmCo saved Rp ~13 bn/yr across two small covered-lagoon PLTBg, and DSNG saved Rp 30.9 bn in 2023 at one site.
  - Biogas can run 24 h, including off-processing hours when the steam turbine is down. Gas storage between the 20-h processing day and night lets it serve housing, the kernel plant and start-up.
- **Avoided pond and LA O&M:**
  - Flatbed LA ≈ Rp 1.2–2.0 bn/yr.
  - Desludging ≈ Rp 0.2–0.6 bn/yr averaged.
  - Pond upkeep and monitoring ≈ Rp 0.2–0.4 bn/yr.
  - **Total ≈ Rp 1.6–3.0 bn/yr (USD 90–170k), i.e. Rp 6,000–11,000/t FFB (USD 0.33–0.62/t FFB).**
- **Regulatory tail risk:**
  - The LA legal basis was revoked in 2021.
  - KLHK floated a ban on LA (2024) and tighter standards (Dec 2024).
  - Enforcement cases exist.
  - A closed AnMBR with low-COD permeate hedges against a forced switch to river-discharge standards, which would require a costly polishing plant.
- **PLN export:** the ≤ 1 MW band (10.18 × F ≈ 11.2 USc/kWh, Rp ~2,000/kWh) is attractive. ~0.96 MW net × ~6,000–7,000 h ≈ 5.8–6.7 GWh ≈ **USD 0.65–0.75 M/yr** at the ceiling. This can carry most of the capex, so the mill fee can be near zero.
- **Freed land:** ~6–9 ha. This is a modest Rp 0.2–0.3 bn/yr as planted area, or Rp 1–2.7 bn one-off. **It is a supporting argument, not a pillar.**

### 2. Where the case is weak (state these honestly in the plan)
1. **The ponds are cheap.** Capex was sunk long ago (≈ Rp 1–1.6 bn) and cash opex excluding LA is only tens of millions of Rp per year. Mills chose ponds because they are cheap. If the mill already has LA, it also gets fertiliser value (Rp 60–105 M/yr) that a closed system may remove, unless the permeate is still land-applied.
2. **Mill electricity during processing is almost free** (fibre/shell turbine). Captive biogas only displaces **diesel** in off-processing hours and start-up, and the kernel plant if it is diesel-driven. **Diesel use is mill-specific: 0.3–1.0 L/t FFB.** A mill already on PLN pays Rp 1,115/kWh, so displacing PLN saves little.
3. **The PLN PPA is the risk hinge.**
   - The Perpres numbers are **ceilings**; prices are negotiated.
   - The excess-power route (IUPTLS) caps at 7.44 USc with no escalation.
   - PPA and interconnection processes are slow, and the year 11+ tariff drops to 6.11 USc.
   - Power wheeling is still not law.
   - A third party selling power directly to the mill may need an IUPTLU with a wilayah usaha, so **the BOO contract must be structured as a treatment/O&M service or biogas sale.**
4. **AnMBR capex is unproven at this scale.** Indonesian lagoon/CSTR references are USD 2.1–3.5 M/MW; membranes and thermophilic heating add cost and O&M (fouling, membrane replacement). A low or zero mill fee requires the PLN tariff near the ceiling **and** a high capacity factor. The Bangka case (IRR 6.75%, NPV < 0) shows POME supply and capacity factor can sink the project.
5. **Adoption evidence cuts both ways.** Fewer than 10% of Indonesian mills capture methane, versus 38% in Malaysia where capture is mandated. Without a mandate or an LA ban, mills lack a compelling reason. **The pitch should stress diesel savings, ISPO/RSPO GHG requirements, buyer pressure and LA regulatory risk.**
6. **Data quality.** Many status-quo cost figures are old (2012–2014), blog-sourced or Malaysian. A mill interview or quote (desludging contract, LA labour, lab invoices, diesel logs) would sharply raise credibility.

### 3. Recommended framing for the model
- **Status-quo annual cost to the mill:**
  - LA ~Rp 1.5 bn + desludging ~Rp 0.4 bn + pond/monitoring ~Rp 0.3 bn + diesel ~Rp 3.0 bn ≈ **Rp 5.2 bn/yr (≈ USD 290k; ≈ Rp 19,000/t FFB ≈ USD 1.07/t FFB)**.
  - Sensitivity range: Rp 3–8 bn/yr.
  - **Diesel is ~55–60% of this.**
- **BOO offer:**
  - Treatment fee ≤ USD 0.3–0.5/t FFB (Rp 1.5–2.4 bn/yr).
  - Captive biogas power at ≤ Rp 1,500/kWh, i.e. far below diesel.
  - The developer's main revenue is the PLN PPA at ~11 USc/kWh.
- **Expected mill saving: Rp 2–4 bn/yr in the base case. It turns marginal or negative if the mill has little diesel use and keeps cheap LA, so say this openly and show a sensitivity on diesel L/t FFB and PLN tariff.**
